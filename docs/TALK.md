# Talk: 20 minutes, 12 slides
Title: "Congruent dissections of a triangle: runs, windows and the corner"
Audience: mathematicians who know tilings or combinatorial geometry; no Lean knowledge assumed. Marks used on slides: F (Lean theorem), W (written proof), S (search).
Timing: slides 1-2 two minutes, 3-4 four, 5-8 eight (the core), 9 two, 10-11 three, 12 two (slide 3 now carries the reduction and slide 10 three new values and a control (204, 276, 279; 268); the total stays 20 minutes by shortening slide 4 to one minute).

**Slide 1. The problem (1.5 min).** Erdős asked: for which N can some triangle be cut into N congruent triangles. Known: the classical counts n^2, n^2+m^2 and 3n^2 (check against the paper's introduction before giving the talk), and Laczkovich's classification of the possible tiles by commensurability relations. Say: this talk does not solve the problem. Figure: the three classical pictures (square count, sum of two squares, 3n^2).

**Slide 2. What is proved and what is not (1.5 min).** Five items: primes (a prime p is a tile count iff p = 3 or p not 3 mod 4); every N up to 200 (105 values); one theorem about the isosceles target T(g); a family of tilings generalising Herdt's 1125; a census of the non-squarefree N up to 10^6. State the standing: results are Lean 4 theorems unless marked W or S; the formal statements have been compared with the written ones inside the project only, no outside reader yet. Credit now: Paliy published the prime proof first (2026-09-29); ours is independent and formal.

**Slide 3. The target T(g) and where it comes from (3 min).** Tile with sides a = u^2, b = v^2 - u^2, c = uv, coprime u < v < 2u; angles alpha, beta, gamma = 2 alpha, cos alpha = v/2u. Target: isosceles triangle with base angles alpha, scale g, base g b v and legs g b u, so N = b g^2. Herdt's tiling at (2,3), g = 15, N = 1125 is the only one that was known with small g. Question: for which g does T(g) have a tiling. Where the target comes from (Section 2 of the paper, Prop. pair; Row4.P5, ThmC.pair_param): if N copies of a tile with angles (alpha, pi-3alpha, 2alpha) tile a triangle with two angles alpha, the two signed-direction functionals give integers M = X/(a+c), M' = X/(c-a) (X the leg) with N = MM', M and M' of the parity of N, 3M < M'; then g = gcd(M'-M, M'+M)/2, u = (M'-M)/2g, v = (M'+M)/2g, M = gd, M' = g(2u+d), N = g^2(v^2-u^2), and the target is similar to T(g). So 'no tiling of this type for N' means 'no pair of N has a tiled T(g)'. Figure: Figure 1 of the paper (target and tile).

**Slide 4. Side words and the fan (1 min).** Every side of T(g) is a word in the edges a, b, c of the tiles that meet it. The fan lemma (no regime hypothesis, ours, F): at a point on a side the tile angles add to pi in only one way, so the edge types along a side are constrained; the paper's lemma on side words then gives each side a word of a fixed shape. Say what a reader should keep: a side is a short word, and a run is a maximal block of consecutive c-edges.

**Slide 5. Runs and the rigidity window (2.5 min).** The method is Beeson and Roșu's: a block of consecutive c-edges, stopped at its far end (the end is a vertex of the target, or the next edge there has the angle gamma at that point), forces a quadratic patch of tiles; inside the patch lengths must be rigid, meaning no second decomposition of a segment of length w a, w b or w c. The window is the number of edges up to which rigidity holds. They have one window k. For this tile it splits (Lemma rigid, proved on the slide in three lines from the relation lattice (y'u+vs, uy', -us-y'v)): (RA) w a is pure for w <= v-1 (first coincidence v a = u c); (RB) w b is pure for w <= u-1 (u b = d(a+c)); (RC) w c has no b for w <= v-1 and is pure for w <= u-1. Say which half is used where: the corner argument uses only RA and RC without b, both window v. Fan lemma: where Beeson and Roșu use gamma > pi/2 (at most one gamma in a half-plane; no gamma in the sector alpha+beta; sector filling), the fan lemma gives the same for every v/u (gamma > pi/2 iff v/u < sqrt 2). Credit line: adapted from their Lemmas 30, 32, 33, 34.

**Slide 6. Lemma K, the corner start (3 min; the proof on the board).** Statement: at a base corner O of T(g), g >= 2, a c-block on a side Sigma starting at O (tile with alpha at O, c on Sigma, b on the other side Sigma') and stopped at its far end has at least v edges. Proof in four lines: induction H(w) = the triangle U x(w,0) x(0,w) is quadratically tiled. (i) The ray UP is a wall beyond P: in the interior case this needs a tile across the ray and (RB) for w b, window u; at a corner UP lies on the side Sigma', which is a wall, and P is interior to Sigma' because w b < |Sigma'|. (ii) The diagonal PQ (length w a) is pure by (RA), so the far row is a row of nablas (fan: at most one gamma). (iii) The alpha-sectors on PQ are filled by translates, or a b-edge runs east: that is the trapezoid lemma, whose proof is on the next backup slide (R is interior by (RC) without b, the column of nablas by (RA), then push s to s+1). (iv) At w = n the stop gives an alpha-tile with a b or c edge on PQ, against (RA). What K contributes: it removes the hypothesis 'a tile across the ray', which cannot hold at a base corner. It is an accident of the proof that only window v is needed; Theorem F uses only 'at least u'. Figure: Figure 3.

**Slide 7. From K to Theorem F (2 min).** Counting identity (proved on the slide): along a leg with k = p+q+r edges, each of the k-1 junctions carries exactly one beta (fan); edges a and c have one beta end each, b none; so mu_beta = q - 1 + (beta at the first end) + (beta at the last end). The apex has one alpha-tile and one beta-tile (different tiles), so exactly one leg A has alpha at both ends, hence q >= 1, m <= g-1, and its word has md <= (g-1)d < u edges c. Lemma 34 gives at least u edges c on A: contradiction. Its proof has four situations for the run R = (backward)^k (forward)^l: (J) an alpha|alpha junction inside R (a beta-tile lies across one of the two b-rays: window u); (E1) R starts at an interior gamma-stop (a beta-tile lies across the b-ray: window u); (E2) R starts at the apex (same, window u); (E3) R starts at the base corner (Lemma K). Theorem F: no tiling of T(g) when (g-1)d < u. Case gd < u is a special case. Corollary: T(2) for every tile of the family; v = u+1: none for g < v. Lean: ThmDCount.counting_identity_gen, ThmDApex.apex_tiles_distinct, ThmDFrameLeg.leg_c_count_S1/S2 (real scale G > 1: a positioned leg, from a base corner to the apex or back, has u <= number of c-edges; no hypothesis on the angle at the apex end, so either leg; the base is not covered). Be exact about marks: that count is F; the pigeonhole is F as a statement on lists (ThmDPig.pig); that the run itself has at least u edges is the written form (W), because the Lean proof is by contradiction and never states the run; the base is a written remark (W). Theorem F uses only the count.

**Slide 8. Scope and controls (1.5 min).** Theorem F is for integer g >= 2 with (g-1)d < u and says nothing beyond: with d = 1 already g = u+1, e.g. (2,3), g = 3 (N = 45); with d >= 2 the first are (3,5), g = 3 (N = 144, a square, so a tile count by other tilings), (4,7), g = 3, (5,8), g = 3, (5,7), g = 4 are not decided by it (exact list from (g-1)(v-u) >= u, by N: 45 at (2,3), g=3; 80, (2,3), g=4; 112, (3,4), g=4; 125, (2,3), g=5; 144, (3,5), g=3; 175, (3,4), g=5; 180, (2,3), g=6; 225, (4,5), g=5; all of these N are tile counts by other families; 528 at (4,7), g=4 is a tile count: 33*4^2, with m = 4 a seed of the semigroup generated by 4 and 9; the first undecided census values with such a target are 297 at (4,7), g=3 and 351 at (5,8), g=3, then 816 at (7,10), g=4). A scan (S, not used in any proof) of the side words under the fan rule, Lemma 34 and Lemma K over u <= 8, g <= 4 rejects exactly the triples with (g-1)d < u; that is a statement about the scan, not a sharpness theorem. Controls, honestly: the known tilings (g = 15, 35, 63 from Herdt and our family; g = 183 is Laczkovich's construction at (2,3), not checked tile by tile here; all with v | g) have (g-1)d >= u, so they do not test Theorem F's hypothesis; they test the lemmas (fan at every point; stopped runs longer than u). Say what is not claimed: nothing for (g-1)d >= u, and no claim that the bound n >= v of Lemma K is attained or optimal.

**Slide 9. Herdt's tiling is a family (2.5 min).** The 11 pieces: seven triangles similar to the tile (scales uvD, u^2 D, bD, tau u b, tau u^3, tau u^2 v, tau v b) and four brick parallelograms (bricks of two tiles); Figure 4 and Table 1 of the paper give the vertices in oblique coordinates. They fit iff two equations hold in six positive integers (D, tau, n1, a1, n2, a2): (E1) b a1 + uv n1 + D u^4 = tau v b^2 (the base closes) and (E2) b a2 + v n2 + tau u^4 = D v b (the side BC closes); g = v(D + tau v). On the board: vb*E1 + u^4*E2 gives tau * Vp = (positive), Vp = (v^2-u^2)^3 v^2 - u^8, so a solution needs Vp > 0, and the explicit solution (a1 = uvVp, n1 = bVp, a2 = vVp, n2 = bVp, tau = 2bv(u^4+buv), D = 2Vp + 2u^4(u^4+buv)) shows it suffices; g0 = 2v(Vp + (u^4+buv)(u^4+bv^2)). Formal: herdt_family (the pieces fit given E1, E2) and herdt_uniform (the explicit g0). Instances: (3,5), g = 35, 19,600 tiles; (4,7), g = 63, 130,977 tiles. Be exact: no new tile count below 10^6 (1125 = 33^2 + 6^2, 19,600 = 140^2, 130,977 = 33*63^2 with 63 in the semigroup generated by 4 and 9). The controls (2,3) -> 1125, 19,600, 130,977 test the construction, not Theorem F's hypothesis. Computed, not proved: that the incidence system has no other solution (u <= 11), and that the domain coincides with Laczkovich's on 127 pairs (no explanation). Credit: instance Herdt's, published by Beeson; the family ours. Excluded: (u, u+1), u >= 3.

**Slide 10. The census (1.5 min).** Non-squarefree N up to 10^6 (392,074 values): 199,140 excluded, 102,471 tile counts, 90,463 undecided. The undecided values add up exactly: 60,666 where exactly one obstruction class is the only failing leaf (base-beta M<f: 28,615; W_M: 18,619; gamma=2alpha beyond F: 10,964; theta word exists: 2,468), 24,807 where several obstruction classes fail (largest: G+theta 16,971; S1+S4 3,426), 4,990 involving a minor leaf (2,024 only minor ones). A census value means a verdict backed by a Lean theorem (whose decidable hypothesis a program evaluates) or a kernel-checked tiling, not a floating-point search. Say exactly: 196,273 of the 199,140 exclusions satisfy the decidable hypothesis of one Lean theorem (the final chain, which contains Theorem F as the G test and the word test for the theta targets; 62,150 of them already satisfied the earlier theorem); the other 2,867 need one further formal sharpening of one leaf (2Me < f on the base-beta targets), which the program applies and the chain does not contain. Four concrete values: 204, 268, 276, 279 are not tile counts; each needs Theorem F (for 204 and 276 the pair (6,34), (6,46) with g = 2, u = 7, 10; for 279 the pair (3,93) with g = 3, u = 15, v = 16) and the word test (for every theta instance t e b < f^3, so no word has f edges c), and neither alone decides them in the chain: the kernel proves CN'zETWD for each and fails CN'zETD and CN'zETW (Draft/ThmDChainWitnessW.lean; no_dissection_276, no_dissection_279_w). Figure: Table 2 (the fifteen leaves) and the partition table.
