\title[Companion data paper]{Erd\H{o}s Problem 634, release 6.0, companion data paper: $132$ tile counts by explicit tilings}
\author{Vico Bonfioli}
\date{18 November 2026 (release 6.0; not refereed)}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
A positive integer $N$ is a \emph{tile count} if some triangle can be cut into $N$ congruent triangles (Erd\H{o}s Problem~634).
This companion to the release-6.0 paper lists $132$ integers $N\le10^6$ that were open in the project's census and are shown here
to be tile counts by \emph{explicit tilings}: $57$ obtained by lifting the layout of a known tiling, $15$ by Zhang's nesting
construction, and $60$ by a strip step and a transfer. The tilings were produced by programs and verified by exact rational
checkers; they are \emph{not} checked by the Lean kernel. The problem is not solved here, and no reader outside the project has examined the tilings.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Overview}\label{sec:comp:overview}
\emph{Roadmap.} This paper has three parts: the families of tiles and targets and the constructions (Section~\ref{sec:tilings}), the list of
the $132$ values with the verification statement (Theorem~\ref{thm:tiles132}, Tables~\ref{tab:lift57} and \ref{tab:follow60}), and the effect on the census of
non-squarefree $N\le10^6$ (Section~\ref{sec:comp:census}). The main paper of the release treats the Lean results and the census in full.

\textbf{How to read the labels.} \sS{} means the output of a computer search with exact verification but without a Lean
certificate; \sF{} means a theorem of the Lean~4 library (used here only for the scale ladder). \emph{Machine-verified} is
our word for a tiling that passed the exact checkers. All checking is internal to the project.

\textbf{Notation.} Here the tile is a triangle with sides $(a,b,c)$, $c^2=a^2+ab+b^2$, with an angle $2\pi/3$ opposite $c$
(Section~\ref{sub:til:tiles}); the letters $a,b,c$ are \emph{not} the sides $(ef,f^2-e^2,f^2)$ of the main paper. A tile count is an
$N$ such that some triangle is cut into $N$ copies of one tile; $F_1,F_2,F_3,\mathrm{Iso}_m$ are target shapes defined in
Section~\ref{sub:til:tiles}.


\section{One hundred and thirty-two further tile counts, by explicit tilings}\label{sec:tilings}

\begin{remark}[Standing]\label{rem:til:status}
The tile counts of this section are established by \emph{explicit tilings} produced by programs and checked by
exact rational checkers (mark \sS: computer outcome with exact verification, not a certificate checked by the Lean
kernel). They are not theorems of the Lean library, and they have been checked inside the project only: by two
independent checkers on every value, by three on a subset, and by a rejected negative control (a tiling with its
first tile dropped is rejected). No person outside the project has examined them. The tilings are stored
compressed in the project work items; their construction rules are stated below in a form a reader can redo by hand.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Tiles with a $2\pi/3$ angle}\label{sub:til:tiles}
All tiles in this section are primitive triples $(a,b,c)$ with $c^2=a^2+ab+b^2$, $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$: the angle opposite
$c$ is $2\pi/3$, $\alpha$ is opposite $a$ and $\beta$ opposite $b$, $\alpha+\beta=\pi/3$. Following Zhang
(arXiv 2512.22696, Props.~9--11) and Harries, there are three target shapes (angles in the order of the sides):
\begin{align*}
 F_1&:\ (\alpha,\ \alpha+\beta,\ \alpha+2\beta),& N&=m^2\,b\,(a+b)\quad(\text{sides }k\,(a,\,c,\,a+b),\ k=mb),\\
 F_2&:\ (2\alpha,\ \alpha+\beta,\ 2\beta),& N&=k^2\,(a+2b)(2a+b),\\
 F_3&:\ (\alpha,\ 2\alpha,\ 3\beta),& N&=3(a+b)(a+2b)k^2,
\end{align*}
and an isosceles target $\mathrm{Iso}_m$ with $N=m^2\,b\,(a+2b)$ (the union of an $F_1$ target and a triangle of
$k^2=m^2b^2$ tiles). All counts were checked against the area ($N=\Area(\text{target})/\Area(\tau)$) and by
integer side arithmetic in $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$.

\subsection{Constructions}\label{sub:til:constr}
\paragraph{Strip step $F_1\to F_1$ (project's construction, hand statement).} Let $m\ge2$ and suppose $F_1$ at
scale $m-1$ is tiled. The target $F_1$ at scale $m$ has a corner $P_0$ of angle $\pi/3$ and sides
$P_0P_1=mba\,e_1$, $P_0P_2=mb(a+b)\,e_2$. It is the union of the scale-$(m-1)$ target translated by $ab\,e_1$ and a
\emph{cap}, a quadrilateral with a side $x=mb(a+b)$, which is tiled by one scaled tile with $c^2$ tiles (its
$c$-side on a side of the cap), one scaled tile with $a^2$ tiles, and a lattice parallelogram patch of sides $ab$ and
$x-c^2=ia+jb$ ($i,j\ge0$) cut into $2(\text{cells})$ tiles. Count: $m^2b(a+b)$.
\paragraph{Transfer $F_1\to\mathrm{Iso}$ (project's construction).} Given a tiling of $F_1$ at scale $m$, glue to it
a triangle $\tau'$ with a $2\pi/3$ angle at $P_0$ such that $Q,P_0,P_2$ are collinear; $\tau'$ is the $k^2$-subdivision of
a scaled tile, $k=mb$. The union is isosceles with $N=m^2b(a+b)+m^2b^2$.
\paragraph{Lift tool.} A search program lifts the layout of a known tiling (the $143$-tiling of $(39,49,55)$
by $(3,5,7)$ for $F_2$, and the layout of the project's $F_1$-160 tiling of the tile $(11,24,31)$) to a new tile with a
$2\pi/3$ angle by solving, in exact rational arithmetic, for the sizes of the lattice pieces. Each output is a list
of $N$ triangles checked by the verifiers.
\paragraph{Nesting $F_2\to F_3$ (Zhang's Prop.~11).} An $F_3$ target is obtained from an $F_2$ band tiling of the
project by Zhang's nesting construction; the multiples follow by the scale ladder (a Lean theorem, \texttt{thm:ladder}).
Prop.~11 is due to Zhang; the $F_2$ input and the verified tilings are ours.

\subsection{The three groups}\label{sub:til:groups}
\begin{theorem}[$132$ tile counts; \sS]\label{thm:tiles132}
Each of the following $132$ integers is the number of triangles congruent to the stated tile into which some
triangle is cut. (a) The $57$ values of Table~\ref{tab:lift57}: $48$ of shape $F_1$ and $9$ of shape $F_2$, from the lift tool.
(b) The $15$ values $6552$, $26208$, $58968$, $104832$, $163800$, $235872$, $321048$ (multiples of the primitive $6552$,
tile $(16,5,19)$), $50760$, $203040$, $456840$, $812160$ (from $50760$, $(33,7,37)$), $230880$, $923520$ (from $230880$,
$(56,9,61)$), $770400$ ($(85,11,91)$) and $421740$ (the non-primitive multiple $4\cdot(16,39,49)$), of shape $F_3$.
(c) The $60$ values of Table~\ref{tab:follow60}: $21$ of shape $F_1$ ($17$ by the strip step, $4$ by the lift tool) and
$39$ of shape $\mathrm{Iso}$ (by the transfer).
\end{theorem}

\noindent \emph{In words.} For each of these $132$ values we have written down an actual tiling and checked it by exact
arithmetic; this adds $132$ tile counts, but the tilings are machine-verified, not Lean-checked.
\emph{Example (from Table~\ref{tab:follow60}).} The tile $(16,39,49)$ has $a=16$, $b=39$ ($49^2=16^2+16\cdot39+39^2$). The
value $19305=3^2\cdot39\cdot55$ is an $F_1$ tile count at scale $m=3$; the transfer adds a triangle of $m^2b^2=13689$ tiles,
giving the isosceles count $32994=19305+13689=3^2\cdot39\cdot94$.

\noindent \emph{Verification statement.} For (a) and (c): every tiling was regenerated and checked by
(A) \texttt{bte verify} v2.18.0, (B) an exact checker \texttt{liftchk} written for this purpose (line count $=N$;
each tile congruent to the tile; target equal to the convex hull of all vertices with exactly three corners; area
count; all pairs of tiles with disjoint interiors by a spatial grid; exact angle decomposition), and, for $23$ of (a)
and the $7$ values of (c) with $N\le120{,}000$, (C) the quadratic checker \texttt{tilingcheck} 1.0.0; every value
passes every checker run on it. For (b): the four primitive values $6552$, $50760$, $230880$, $770400$ pass \texttt{bte} and \texttt{f3chk}; \texttt{tilingcheck}
was run on $6552$ and $50760$ only; the multiples follow from the Lean scale ladder
(\texttt{Subdivision.ladderDissection}, \sF). Rooms: \rec{VERIFY\_LIFT55\_2026-11-16}, \rec{VERIFY\_F3\_2026-11-15},
\rec{LIFT\_FOLLOWON\_2026-11-16}. Negative controls: dropping the first tile is rejected by \texttt{liftchk} on all
$117$ of (a),(c) and by \texttt{bte} on a sample. None of the $132$ is in a no-tiling row of the project ledger. All three groups are later than release v5.0
(1 November 2026): the $15$ of (b) were found and checked on 15 November, the $57$ of (a) on 15--16 November and the $60$ of (c)
on 16 November 2026, so none of them is among the $117$ explicit tile counts of v5.0 (a list comparison with the $117$ is to be run before release).

\paragraph{Prior art and credit.} Shapes $F_1,F_2,F_3$ and the nesting are Zhang's (2512.22696) and Harries's
constructions (hand tail $F_1$, $m\ge3R$; $F_2$, $k\ge\lceil3R/2\rceil$); six of the nine $F_2$ values of (a) are
covered by a structural statement of Paliy (nested corner at scale $m$). The $143$-tiling is Herdt's skeleton in our
search. The strip step and the transfer are the project's. The values are tile counts that were open in the project's
census; the statement that they were not previously known rests on the files on disk and on the sources listed in
\texttt{PRIORITY.md}, and should be rechecked online.

\section{Effect on the census}\label{sec:comp:census}
\emph{Roadmap.} We recall what the census counts and show the three steps at which the $132$ tilings enter it.

The census universe is the set of non-squarefree integers $N\le10^6$ ($392{,}074$ values). A value is \emph{decided} if it is
known to be a tile count or is excluded (proved not to be a tile count), and \emph{undecided} otherwise. The programs
are internal and their lists are described in the main paper of the release.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\hline
list & undecided $N\le10^6$ & step\\\hline
2026-11-15c: before the tilings & 57,770 & \\
2026-11-15d: $F_3$ nesting tilings & 57,755 & $-15$\\
2026-11-16b: 57 lift tilings & 57,621 & $-57$\\
2026-11-16g: 60 tilings & 52,045 & $-60$\\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(Steps of the chain that are Lean results of the main paper are omitted from this table, so consecutive lines differ by more than the step shown.)
The three groups lower the undecided count by $15+57+60=132$. The other steps of the chain are Lean results of the main
paper. Since release v5.0 (1 November 2026) the undecided count fell from $62{,}372$ to $52{,}045$, of which
$161$ values are decided by tilings ($24+5+15+57+60$); the $132$ of this paper are those of the last three groups, and $29$
further tilings of 12--14 November 2026 are outside its scope.
